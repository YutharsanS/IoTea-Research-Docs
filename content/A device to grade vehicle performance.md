"The private buses in Sri Lanka are mostly owned by individual owners. Yes, they are owners/companies that have multiple buses, but each bus essentially runs independently. Routes are decided by some government transport authority and route permits per bus are auctioned. There is a lot of corruption in that auction process. Usually, it’s the politically connected that get these.

The driver and the conductor are not employed like normal and paid a fixed salary. They are on a contract with the owner and are entitled to 20% & 15% respectively of total revenue from ticket sales. In reality however, it’s bloody impossible to track the amount of revenue. They just cheat. They just never issue tickets. Therefore owners basically just give the bus away to the driver and conductor to use as they please as long as he gets a fixed amount per month for the bus and the route permits. All this gives the driver and the conductor the incentive either drive really fast, breaking all rules or really slow depending on the situation to maximize the passengers they get.

And no. All the decorations, bling and chrome are not related to gang activity. This is not like South America where people have different tattoos to indicate which gang they belong. Now the owners/drivers/conductors may occasionally have connections to gangs, they may have to pay ransoms to gangs, but they are not like mafia bosses.

The driver and conductors simply treat their bus as their own. They just like to express it artistically. Regulations aren’t really imposed to stop that. They may steel money, take illegal substances and drive but they are also somehow religious and like to express that as well in the bus.

I have also seen foreigners say the Sri Lankan buses drive crazy fast and videos of them holding onto their seat for dear life on YouTube. It’s not that they actually drive that fast in raw Kilometers per Hour (Kmph) scale. The busses are just made from a ladder frame chassis that it shares with dump truck. It’s the sheer shakiness & discomfort that causes you to feel like it goes very fast even when it’s just running at 80 kmph (50mph) which is nothing compared speeds foreigner drive in expressways."
