### 1. Introduction

Road safety and driving discipline in Sri Lanka remain major concerns, particularly due to lane violations, improper use of headlights, unsafe overtaking, roundabout misuse, and inconsistent speed behavior. Public and private vehicles—especially buses operating under revenue-driven incentive structures—often exhibit risky driving patterns that negatively impact overall traffic safety and public trust.

This project proposes a **Vehicle Grading Device (VGD)** that objectively evaluates and grades vehicle driving behavior using sensor data, creating accountability and encouraging safer driving practices.

---

### 2. Problem Statement
- Poor lane discipline and unsafe overtaking on single carriageways
- Improper use of high beams causing visibility hazards
- Reckless or inconsistent speed control, especially in buses
- Lack of transparent monitoring of driver behavior
- Economic incentives that reward unsafe driving to maximize passengers

Current enforcement methods rely heavily on manual policing, which is inconsistent and prone to limitations.

---

### 3. Proposed Solution

Develop a **compact, onboard Vehicle Grading Device** that continuously monitors driving behavior and assigns a **safety grade** to each vehicle or driver.
The system will:
- Collect driving data using sensors and cameras
- Analyze behavior using rule-based logic and AI models
- Generate a **driving score/grade** over time
- Provide feedback to authorities, fleet owners, or insurers

---

### 4. Key Features
- Lane discipline monitoring
- Speed consistency and sudden acceleration/braking detection
- Headlight usage analysis (high beam misuse)
- Unsafe overtaking and tailgating detection
- Time-based behavioral scoring (daily/weekly/monthly)

---

### 5. Impact

- Encourages safer driving through accountability
- Enables data-driven regulation and enforcement
- Helps identify high-risk drivers and vehicles
- Improves public transport safety perception
- Can be used for insurance incentives or fleet management
