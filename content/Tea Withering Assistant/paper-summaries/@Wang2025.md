### Summary
---
- A **non-destructive detection model** for determining the withering degree of fresh tea leaves using image classification confidence. 
- Utilized **YOLOv8s** as the baseline model, optimized by replacing standard convolutions with **Receptive-Field Attention Convolution (RFAConv)** and introducing a **Cross-Stage Feature Fusion Coordinate Attention (C2f_CA)** module to address issues with parameter sharing and feature loss.

### Research Gaps
---
Pre-research Gaps:
- **Subjectivity of Traditional Methods**
- **Limitations of Machine Learning:** Previous machine learning methods (such as SVM) relied on inputting fixed features like color and texture, which limited the model's ability to learn complex features.
- **Deep Learning Optimization:** While YOLO models **==exist==**, the standard YOLOv8s C2f module suffers from minor information loss during feature fusion; this study addresses that gap by integrating Coordinate Attention mechanisms.

### Flow and Limitations
---
**Work Flow:**

1. **Data Acquisition:** Images of **Jin Guan Yin** tea leaves across 13 consecutive time intervals alongside and moisture data were collected hourly using an industrial camera and moisture analyzer under controlled conditions. 
2. **Preprocessing:** Data was augmented via cropping, rotating, and flipping to prevent overfitting, resulting in 2,255 training samples.
3. **Model Optimization:** The YOLOv8s model was modified by adding RFAConv to adjust receptive fields dynamically and C2f_CA to enhance feature fusion and reduce info loss.
4. **Prediction Algorithm:** The model outputs a withering degree by calculating the weighted sum of the classification confidence and the moisture labels.
5. **Evaluation:** The model was tested against external datasets using Rp, RMSEP, and RPD metrics. The experimental results showed that the improved model achieved a **classification accuracy of 92.7%**, an improvement of 0.156 over the initial model. (Made real-time - 5.3 ms.)
	In quantitative evaluations, the model achieved a correlation coefficient (Rp) of 0.9983 and a Root Mean Square Error (RMSEP) of 0.006278, significantly outperforming Partial Least Squares (PLS) and Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) methods.

**Limitations:**
- **Computational Cost:** The improvements increased the model parameters by 0.224 M and floating-point operations (GFLOPs) by 1.2 G, resulting in a slight decrease in inference speed (from 1.4 ms to 5 ms). 
- **Category-Specific Errors:** The model showed larger prediction errors for specific categories (e.g., category 9) because the color and texture of tea leaves change very little during certain continuous time intervals.
- **Scope of Study:** The current model is limited to a **single tea variety** (Jin Guan Yin) and relies on **single-modal analysis** (images only), which may restrict its universal applicability without further research.
