### Summary
---
This study investigates how irradiating tea leaves with specific light wavelengths during the withering process affects the sensory and chemical quality of black tea instead of the "traditional sunlight".

- Methodology: Researchers designed specific withering equipment to treat fresh tea leaves (_Camellia sinensis_ cv. Fudingdabai) with various monochromatic lights (yellow, blue, purple, orange, red, cyan, green, and ultraviolet), as well as white light (control) and darkness. 
	The withered leaves were then processed into black tea through cutting, fermentation, and drying.
- Chemical analysis: Green light was detrimental, causing astringency, resulting in the lowest sensory scores. **Ultraviolet (UV)** light also resulted in strong astringency.

> [!quote]
> Yellow, orange and red light withering significantly improved the aroma and taste, imparting the tea a sweet flavor and a fresh and mellow taste. Tea treated with yellow light was scored highest the sensory scores and showed the highest content in catechins, theaflavins, amino acids and aroma components, followed by the orange and red light treatments.


- **Mechanism:** Possibly photoreceptors in the leaves (such as chlorophyll) absorb specific wavelengths (particularly red, yellow, and blue), promoting photosynthesis and metabolic activities that increase soluble sugars and facilitate the synthesis of amino acids. ==(Researcher's theory)==

### Research Gaps
---
Post-research Gaps:
- Hybrid light: They did try mixing lights but seemingly found no cumulative results.
- Mechanism of sunlight: Does not say how sunlight affects leaves and how this radiation works better in molecular level.
- Genetics: The experiment was limited to the "Fudingdabai" clone. The paper does not address if these light sensitivities vary across different tea cultivars.

### Flow and Limitations
---
**Flow:** 
1. **Equipment Design:** Construction of a custom wooden framework with ventilation and specific lamp installation.
2. **Treatment:** Withering tea shoots under 9 different light conditions plus dark and control groups.
3. **Processing:** Standardized rolling, fermentation (5.5 hours), and drying.
4. **Evaluation:** Sensory evaluation by professional tasters and chemical analysis (HPLC, GC-MS) of the final product.
5. **Statistical Analysis:** ANOVA and LSD tests to determine significance.

**Limitations:**
- **UV Trade-off:** UV light presented a limitation where it increased beneficial polyphenols but degraded the sensory experience by reducing sugars and amino acids, leading to unwanted astringency.
