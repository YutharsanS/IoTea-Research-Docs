### Terms
---
- **Falling rate period**: The phase of the withering process where the rate of moisture removal from the tea leaves decreases over time. This period is characterized by internal mass transfer controlling the overall drying process.

### Summary
---
- This research found that the withering process occurs entirely within a **falling rate period**, meaning no constant rate of drying was observed. The moisture transfer is primarily controlled by internal mass transfer resistance.
- To describe this behavior, five mathematical models were tested against experimental data: Lewis, Henderson & Pabis, Logarithmic, Page, and the Two-term model. 
- The **Two-term model** was identified as the most accurate, providing the highest coefficient of determination and the lowest standard error. 
- The study calculated the **effective diffusivity** of water in tea leaves, which increased exponentially with temperature, and determined the **activation energy** required for moisture diffusion to be **1477.75 kJ/kg**.

### Research Gaps
---
* **Cultivar Specificity:** The study focused exclusively on the **TRI 2025** cultivar (only for SL).
* **Airflow Rate Constraints:** Assumption of consistent air flow rates.
* **Surface Water Effects:** The study used leaves **free from surface water**. In commercial practice, leaves may arrive with wet surface moisture, which can introduce a brief constant-rate period that would not captured in this thin-layer model.

### Flow and Limitations
---
#### Workflow
1. **Preparation:** 50g samples are spread thinly to ensure uniform exposure.
2. **Stabilization:** The chamber runs empty for 3 hours to stabilize dry and wet bulb temperatures before leaf insertion.
3. **Data Logging:** An electronic balance logs weight changes via an **RS-232 serial cable** to a computer at constant intervals until a constant weight is reached.
4. **Analysis:** Experimental moisture ratios are fitted to the Two-term model using non-linear regression.

#### Limitations
* **Thin instead of Thick Bed:** A thin layer eliminates the effects of leaf compaction and air temperature/humidity changes that occur as air passes through a thick bed in a commercial trough.
* **Equipment Capacity:** The constant rate period *might be* a limitation of the **==air supply's evaporative capacity==** rather than a property of the tea leaf itself.

> [!quote] Industrial machines are not that better??
> Temple et al. (2000) reported that a single falling rate period was enough to describe the whole process of tea. However, using some industrial equipment a constant rate period could be observed due to limited evaporative capacity of the air. In such cases, constant rate period was a property of air supply rather than a drying property of the material.

* **Temperature Range:** Higher temperatures might accelerate drying further but risk damaging the quality.

### Economic Impact: Cost Reduction & Profit Increase
---
* **Precision Withering:** Prevents the excessive use of thermal energy, reducing fuel costs.
* **Time Optimization:** The model predicts how decreasing RH or increasing temperature shortens withering time. Faster turnover allows factories to process more leaf volume during peak cropping months without investing in new troughs.
* **Quality Preservation:** Maintaining air temperature below  at later stages (as suggested by the researched data) preserves leaf quality.
