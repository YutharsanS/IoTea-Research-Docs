RoHS certification ensures electrical and electronic products comply with European Union (EU) directives restricting hazardous substances like lead, mercury, and cadmium, protecting human health and the environment by limiting harmful chemicals in e-waste and during product lifecycle. While not a mandatory "certification mark," it's a compliance requirement for selling electronics in the EU and many other regions, achieved through documentation or testing to confirm products are free from restricted substances above set thresholds, notes [TÜV SÜD](https://www.tuvsud.com/en/customer-hub/ps-cert/certification-mark-for-rohs). 

Key Aspects of RoHS

- **What it is:** A directive restricting hazardous substances in electrical and electronic equipment (EEE).
- **Goal:** To reduce risks to health and the environment from hazardous materials in e-waste.
- **Restricted Substances:** Includes Lead (Pb), Mercury (Hg), Cadmium (Cd), Hexavalent Chromium (Cr6+), Polybrominated Biphenyls (PBBs), Polybrominated Diphenyl Ethers (PBDEs), and phthalates (DEHP, BBP, DBP, DIBP).
- **Scope:** Applies to a wide range of products, from large appliances to small electronics and IT equipment.
- **Compliance:** Achieved through either a documentary approach (IEC 63000) or analytical testing, often requiring documentation and due diligence.
- **Mandatory for:** Sale in the EU and increasingly other markets like China, India, and parts of the US. 

Why it Matters

- **Environmental Protection:** Reduces hazardous materials in landfills and during recycling.
- **Health & Safety:** Protects workers and consumers from exposure to heavy metals and other toxic chemicals.
- **Market Access:** Required for manufacturers and importers to sell electronics in regulated markets.
