
[[@Botheju2010|Simulation of Trough Withering of Tea using One Dimensional Heat and Mass Transfer Finite Difference Model]] is about a mathematical model that predicts the moisture content. They used **finite difference methods** and **layered simulation** (see further explanations in paper summary) and the results of the predictions were tested with 800kg of tea and proved.

> [!warning] Model assumptions
> There were many assumptions taken for the model:
> - Temperature and relative humidity are uniform in the horizontal direction
> - Adiabatic reversible process


Might be an improved version of above (same authors and same topic, but different experimental chambers). [[@Botheju2011|Thin layer drying characteristics of fresh tea leaves]] says about the **suitable** moisture content level in withering and that there are 2 stages where the moisture will continuously decrease over time which they found in **their constant climatic chamber**. This might help in decision making for a *monitoring* system (if we go through the plan of a decision helper). Also it may have been implicitly mentioned that the machines do not provide the correct/ efficient method of drying.

> [!trouble]  Does not say which tea leaves were used for testing unlike in "Modeling Trough Withering System"


[[@Weerawardena2018|Development of a Mathematical Procedure for Controlling Air Flow Rate in Tea Withering]] seems to have actually implemented an **==IoT solution== to control heat flow rate**. They used a *Raspberry Pi 3 model B* to run the mathematical model and reduced the amount of energy used in factories considerably for withering.
